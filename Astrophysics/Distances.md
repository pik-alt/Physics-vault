
Eratosthenes measured very accurately the circumference of the Earth using the different angles that shadow's made in different cities.

The Greeks also figured out how to tell how far the moon orbited, as during a lunar eclipse, the shape of the shadow that the earth makes from the sunlight is very close to a cylinder, and so you know how wide it is.
You can then time how long the moon spends in that shadow and that ratio is equal to the period of the moon (~29 days) to the circumference of the orbit.


## Parallax

How far an object is from you, $d$, can be calculated if you move some baseline distance, $b$, away and measure the change in angle, $\theta$.


![[parallax example.png]]

$\tan\theta = \frac{b}{d} \therefore d=\frac{b}{\tan\theta}$

Since we're usually dealing with very small angles, we can say that $\tan\theta \approx \theta$ to get $d \approx \frac{b}{\theta}$


To get the baseline $b$ on Earth, we usually use the orbit around the sun.
