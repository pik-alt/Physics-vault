
If we plot a diagram of the [[Brightness Distances|absolute magnitude]] of a star against its colour as seen on Earth, we would see that stars occupy very distinct regions:
![[HR Diagram.png|397]]


The main narrow band is called the "**Main Sequence**"

At the top, the red and bright stars are the red giants and supergiants.

The stragglers at the bottom that are blue and dim are the dwarf stars.

Since stars can be approximated as black bodies, we can use the [[Light|black body formula]] relating luminosity to temperature:

$L=4\pi r^{2}\sigma T^{4}$

Take the log of both sides to get:
$\log L \propto 4\log T$

## Classification

Stars fit into categories which go, in decreasing temperature:

O, B, A, F, G, K, M, (L, T)

Each category is also split into sub-categories ranging from 0-9, again in decreasing temperature.

e.g. B0 is hotter than B5 which is hotter than A0.

L and T are brown dwarf stars.

The sun is a G2 star.


When looking at the spectra of stars, we see that they follow a general black body shape with some superposed absorption line points, where the matter in the star absorbs photons of that particular frequency and emits it elsewhere, making that frequency dimmer.
This was used to show that stars are mostly Hydrogen with a little bit of Helium.

## Energy Levels

Due to quantum physics where an electron has to be a standing wave with an integer number of de-broglie wavelengths, we can see the range of valid radii from the nucleus of a hydrogen atom.
This means that we can calculate the potential energy at each energy level:

$E_{n} = -\frac{13.6}{n^{2}}eV$
