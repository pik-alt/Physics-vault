**An ideal body that absorbs all photons that hit it (meaning none are reflected) and emits all wavelengths of photon is called a Blackbody**
These are purely hypothetical.

The radiation they emit is called **blackbody radiation**.

The amount of blackbody radiation emitted depends only on temperature and surface area.

![[Blackbody spectrum.png|421]]

*An example of a blackbody radiation spectrum at different temperatures*

The radiation of a certain wavelength is described with Planck's law:

$B(T) = \frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5} \left( \frac{1}{\exp\left( \frac{hc}{\lambda k} \right)-1} \right)$

$h = \text{Planck's constant = }6.63\times 10^-34$
$c = \text{speed of light}$
$k = \text{Boltzmann constant } = 1.4 \times 10 ^-23$

B is in units of Flux per metre wavelength, $Wm^{-2}m^{-1}$


The peak wavelength, $\lambda_{\text{peak}}$ occurs at:
$\frac{2.897\times 10^{-3}}{T}$

The total flux, gotten by integrating the curve to find the area under, is defined as:

$F=\sigma T^4 \text{   }Wm^{-2}$

$\sigma$ is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant: $=5.67\times 10^{-8}$

For a spherical object, like a star, the total luminosity is the total flux times the surface area:
$L=A\times F = 4\pi R^2\sigma T^4$

**Colour Temperature: Looking at a very specific range of detected wavelengths from an object and fitting a blackbody curve to them**

**Effective Temperature: Looking at the whole range of wavelengths emitted by an object and fitting a blackbody curve to the whole spectrum**
