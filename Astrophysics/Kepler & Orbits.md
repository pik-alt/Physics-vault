
## Kepler's laws:
1. Planets orbit the sun in ellipses with the sun at one of two focii.
2. Planets sweep out equal areas in equal times
3. The period $P$ and semi-major axis $a$ of the orbit are related through:
   $P^2 = a^3$


Examples and explanations.

### First Law:

Ellipses have two axis, a major axis of length $2a$ and a minor one of length $2b$.
Ellipses have a property called Eccentricity, $e$, equivalent to:

$e=\sqrt{ 1-\frac{b^2}{a^2} }$

The foci of an ellipse are located at a distance $ea$ from the centre.

### Second Law:

We can connect this law to [[Gravity]]. 

Angular momentum $L = mrv$

$v=r \frac{d\theta}{dt}$ (for a circle)

$L=mr^2 \frac{d\theta}{dt}$

and then do some shit:

$\frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2} = 0 \therefore \frac{d\theta}{dt} = 0$

There is no angular component of acceleration, so acceleration must be radial.

## Binary Orbits

Kepler's laws only apply for $m \ll M$, such that the central object (e.g. the sun) can be assumed stationary.

When this is not the case, two body's orbit around each other around a fixed centre of mass, defined by:

$x_{\text{com}} = \frac {m_{1}x_{1} + m_{2}x_{2}}  {m_{1} + m_{2}}$

where $x_{1} \text{ and } x_{2}$ are the positions in space of the planets(?)

Both orbits have the same period.

Doing some re-arrangement shit gets you to:

$P^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{G}\left( \frac{r^3}{m_{1} + m_{2}} \right)$
